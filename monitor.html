The Emerald Tree Monitor

Learn about the Emerald Tree Monitor!

Information

The emerald tree monitor (Varanus prasinus)[1] is a lizard belonging to the Varanidae family. They grow around 75–100cm (30–39 in) in length and weigh around 300g.

Habitat

Emerald tree monitors are native to New Guinea and adjacent islands, including the Torres Strait Islands.

Diet

Emerald tree monitors are primarily insectivores, but if presented with other prey they will also eat:

  • Insects
  • Small Reptiles
  • Small Birds
  • Eggs

Predators

Green tree monitors have no known predators as adults; they act as apex predators within their canopy habitats.

Conservation

Emerald tree monitors are currently evaluated as "Least Concern" on the IUCN Red List. While not immediately threatened with extinction, trade in the species is closely monitored to prevent population decline.

Behavior

The emerald tree monitor exhibits primitive social behavior. They live in small family groups made up of a dominant male, several females, and a few subordinate males and juveniles.
