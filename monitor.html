The Emerald Tree Monitor

Learn about the Emerald Tree Monitor!

Information

The emerald tree monitor (Varanus prasinus)[1] is a large lizard belonging to the Varanidae family. Around 75-100cm (30-39 in) and weighs 300g

Habitat

Emerald tree monitors are native to the Pacific islands, including Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands

Diet

The emerald tree monitors are insectivores, meaning they only eat insects, but is presented will eat small repitiles, birds, and eggs.

  • Insects
  • Small Reptiles
  • Small Birds
  • Eggs

Predators

Green tree monitors have no known predators as adults, they are the apex predators in their habitats.

Conservation

Emerald tree monitors are not currently listed on the IUCN Red List, but they are "least concern." They are not necessarily threatened with extinction, but may become so unless trade is closely controlled.

Behavior

The emerald tree monitor exhibits primitive social behavior. The lizards live in small groups made up of a dominant male, several females and a few other males and juveniles.
